# How the Agents Collaborate

**Last updated:** 2026-08-18
**Read this to understand:** how a raw idea becomes a decision Maya can act on.

---

## The core principle

> **One idea in. One recommendation out.**

Maya should never receive eight separate opinions and have to reconcile them herself. The Chief of Staff does that work first.

---

## The full evaluation workflow

Used for a new product, offer, market, or significant opportunity.

```
   MAYA has an idea or a goal
            │
            ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  1. CHIEF OF STAFF  │  Clarifies the goal. Decides which agents
   │     scopes it       │  are needed. Writes the assignment brief.
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  2. MARKET          │  Is there real demand? Who wants it?
   │     RESEARCHER      │  What do competitors do? What's the gap?
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             │  ── if demand is weak, STOP HERE and report back ──
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  3. PRODUCT &       │  Is it educationally sound? Buildable?
   │     CURRICULUM      │  Age-appropriate? On-brand? Supply list?
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  4. FINANCE         │  What does it cost? What's the margin?
   │                     │  What price does it need? Cash required?
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             │  ── if margin doesn't work, STOP and report back ──
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  5. SALES           │  What's the offer? Who's it sold to,
   │                     │  through what funnel, with what journey?
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  6. SOCIAL MEDIA    │  How do the right people find out?
   │                     │  Campaign, content, hooks, calendar.
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  7. OPERATIONS      │  Can we actually make, pack, and ship it
   │                     │  at this volume, on time, at this cost?
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             │  ── if we can't fulfill it, STOP and report back ──
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  8. CUSTOMER        │  How do we onboard them and keep them?
   │     EXPERIENCE      │  What will they ask? Why would they leave?
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │  9. CHIEF OF STAFF  │  Combines everything into ONE
   │     synthesizes     │  recommendation. Flags approvals.
   └─────────┬───────────┘  Surfaces disagreements with a trade-off.
             ▼
   ┌─────────────────────┐
   │   MAYA DECIDES      │  Approve · Reject · Request changes
   └─────────┬───────────┘
             ▼
      Logged in company/decision-log.md
```

## The three stop-gates

The workflow **stops early** and reports back — it does not push on — when:

| Gate | Held by | Stops when |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand gate** | Market Researcher | There's no evidence anyone wants this |
| **Margin gate** | Finance Manager | The numbers don't work at any plausible price |
| **Fulfillment gate** | Operations Manager | We physically cannot deliver it as promised |

Failing a gate is a **success** — it saved money that would otherwise have been spent finding out the hard way. The Chief of Staff reports the stop with the reasoning, and Maya decides whether to proceed anyway.

## The three shorter workflows

Most work is not a full nine-step evaluation.

### Workflow A — Weekly rhythm
```
Maya states the week's focus
        ▼
Chief of Staff sets 3–5 priorities → tasks/master-task-list.md
        ▼
Assigns each to ONE owning agent
        ▼
Agents work and file their output
        ▼
Chief of Staff writes the Friday founder briefing
        ▼
Maya reviews, approves, redirects
```

### Workflow B — Single-question request
```
Maya asks a specific question
        ▼
Chief of Staff routes it to the ONE agent who owns it
        ▼
That agent answers, flags anything needing approval
        ▼
Straight back to Maya
```
*Do not convene the whole team for a question with one owner.*

### Workflow C — Launch a kit topic
```
Product Manager confirms the kit is ready and gives real specs
        ▼
Finance confirms price and margin        ─┐
Operations confirms stock and ship date  ─┤ these run together
                                          ─┘
        ▼
Sales builds the offer and the funnel
        ▼
Social builds the campaign around the offer
        ▼
Customer Experience prepares onboarding and the FAQ
        ▼
Chief of Staff assembles the launch plan with a dated checklist
        ▼
MAYA APPROVES before anything is published or sent
```

## Handoff rules

1. **Hand off facts, not vibes.** A handoff includes the actual numbers, files, and labels — not "research says people like sharks."
2. **Label everything you pass on.** The receiving agent inherits your labels. An unlabeled number becomes someone else's invented fact.
3. **Name what's missing.** Say explicitly what you could not determine, so the next agent doesn't assume it was checked.
4. **Never overwrite another agent's file.** Ask them to revise, or flag the conflict to the Chief of Staff.
5. **Point to the file.** "See `finance/cogs/sharks.md`" beats restating numbers that will drift out of sync.

---

## When agents disagree

Agents do **not** argue, negotiate, or quietly compromise. The Chief of Staff:

1. States each position in one sentence.
2. Names the real trade-off — usually money, time, risk, or brand.
3. Gives its own recommendation, with reasoning.
4. **Asks Maya to decide.**

**Example:**
> **Disagreement:** Product wants a glass specimen jar; Operations says glass will break in shipping.
> **Trade-off:** Glass feels like real science equipment and is more on-brand. Plastic survives shipping and costs less, but feels more like a craft kit.
> **Recommendation:** Order 20 of each and ship a test batch before committing.
> **Maya decides.**

Outcome goes to `company/decision-log.md`.

---

## The escalation rule

Any agent escalates immediately, without waiting for a weekly cycle, when:
- A safety issue with a kit appears
- The financial model shows the business may be unprofitable
- Cash flow projects a shortfall
- A plan exceeds fulfillment capacity
- Churn is rising
- Something contradicts a decision Maya already made

**Immediately means immediately.**
