## C. The six findings that matter

### 🔴 1. The subscription is sold under the wrong brand — this is the big one

A parent searches "marine biology kit for kids," lands on **Jr. Marine Biologist**, decides they want the monthly kit — and is handed off to a **differently-named website** to buy it.

That handoff happens at the single moment of highest purchase intent. Every extra step and every moment of "wait, is this the same company?" costs conversions. Your stated #1 business goal is *increasing paid subscriptions*, and the path to subscribing currently runs through a brand-name change.

**This is not a criticism of the workaround** — GoDaddy genuinely can't do subscriptions, so the workaround was the right call to keep selling. But it is now the most expensive structural problem in the business.

**Options to evaluate** (Finance and Sales should cost these out before any decision):
- **(a)** Move the store to a platform that does both one-time and subscription billing on the Jr. Marine Biologist domain
- **(b)** Keep the split, but make the handoff seamless — matching visual design, an explicit "Under Blue Waters is our parent company" line, and no surprise
- **(c)** Consolidate everything under one brand name

> **This needs your decision, not the team's.** It touches brand, cost, and time. Nobody should act on it without you.

### 🟠 2. Your credentials are being under-sold

The site reportedly describes you as "a passionate marine biologist and mom." True — but incomplete. You are also an **Eckerd College marine science and environmental education graduate, a scuba instructor, a boat captain**, and you have been running an ocean adventure operation since **2009**.

Almost no competitor in the children's science-kit category can claim any of that. It's the one advantage that cannot be copied, and it's currently mostly invisible.

### 🟠 3. Free US shipping still makes margin the urgent question

> **CORRECTED 2026-08-18.** This finding originally cited a plush toy and a printed magazine, based on a search-result summary. **Maya confirmed neither is in the kit.** That materially reduces the cost concern — the corrected version stands below.

Free US shipping on every plan, combined with printed cards, 5 activities, and a personalized certificate, at roughly **$39.99–$41.99 per kit**, still leaves margin unquantified. Shipping alone on a physical kit is usually the largest single line after materials.

**Nobody on this team can tell you whether you are making money on each kit.** Finance can answer it same-day once you provide supply costs and your actual shipping cost per box.

⚠️ **Separately, worth 5 minutes of your time:** check whether any page on underbluewaters.com actually promises a plush toy or a magazine. If a page does, customers are being promised items that never arrive — a refund and bad-review risk. If no page does, the search engine simply got it wrong and nothing needs fixing.

### 🟠 4. The month-to-month price is hard to find

Search surfaced both prepay bundles but **not** the monthly rate. That may mean it isn't prominently displayed. Month-to-month is usually the lowest-commitment entry point and the biggest source of new subscribers — if it's buried, you're losing the easiest yeses.

### 🟠 5. Under Blue Waters' reputation now affects kit sales

Because checkout runs through Under Blue Waters, that brand's public reviews now influence kit conversion. One publicly visible negative review (Yahoo Local) reported a child who "primarily just swam and got sunburned" and questioned the $79/day value.

**One review is not a pattern**, and nothing should be concluded from it. But it is worth knowing that a parent researching before buying may see it.

### 🟢 6. Your product content is genuinely strong

Illustrated cards, real experiments, a personalized certificate, and a 12-kit reward path is a well-constructed product. The "24 topics covering marine ecosystems, creatures, current research, careers, and ocean conservation" framing is on-brand and clearly not a generic craft box. **The product does not appear to be the problem. The path to buying it does.**
