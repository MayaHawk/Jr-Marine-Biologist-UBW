# Brand Voice Guide — Junior Marine Biologist

**Last updated:** 2026-08-18
**Who must follow this:** every agent that writes anything a customer could read.

---

## The one-sentence test

> **Does this sound like a real marine scientist who is genuinely excited to show a kid something amazing about the ocean?**

If yes, ship it for approval. If it sounds like a marketing department, rewrite it.

---

## We are

**CONFIRMED brand attributes:** exciting · trustworthy · educational · adventurous · polished · colorful · created by real marine educators

## We are NOT

**CONFIRMED** — Not a generic soft craft subscription. Concretely, that means we avoid:

| Avoid | Because |
|---|---|
| "Cute crafty activity for little ones" | We teach real science, not crafts |
| Pastel, soft, sleepy language | The ocean is thrilling; the brand should be too |
| Vague wonder-words with no content ("magical sea friends!") | Trustworthy means specific |
| Talking down to children | Kids can handle real vocabulary |
| Corporate marketing voice | We are a scientist, not a brand team |
| Fear-based parenting guilt | We sell excitement, not anxiety |

---

## Voice rules

1. **Lead with the real thing.** Name the actual animal, structure, or process. "A shark's skin is covered in tiny tooth-shaped scales called denticles" beats "Sharks are so cool!"
2. **Use real science vocabulary, then explain it.** Denticle, cephalopod, echinoderm, photic zone. Children love the big word when you give them the meaning. This is a core brand differentiator.
3. **Write to the parent, excite the child.** Most copy is read by an adult deciding to buy, for a child who will decide to care.
4. **Short sentences. Active voice. Concrete nouns.**
5. **Adventurous, not hyped.** Wonder comes from the fact, not from exclamation marks. One "!" per paragraph, maximum.
6. **Human, never robotic.** No "delve," "elevate," "unlock," "embark on a journey," "in today's world," "game-changer."
7. **Screen-free is a feature.** Say it plainly for the audience that wants it.
8. **Accuracy is the brand.** A single wrong marine fact damages the trust that everything else rests on. Flag anything uncertain for verification.

---

## Founder voice vs. brand voice

- **Brand voice** ("we"): warm, expert, welcoming. Used for product pages, kit copy, FAQs, most social.
- **Founder voice** ("I"): first-person Maya. Used for story content, behind-the-scenes, teaching moments, homeschool content. Warmer, more personal, allowed to be imperfect and real.

Agents must be told which voice to use. If unclear, ask.

## Writing for children (ages ~4–12)

This is a wide range. Always specify the band:

| Band | Approach |
|---|---|
| **4–6** | Read aloud by an adult. Sensory, simple. One idea per sentence. |
| **7–9** | Independent readers. Real vocabulary with immediate plain-language definitions. |
| **10–12** | Wants to be taken seriously. Real science, real data, real consequences. |

**NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT** — Is each kit written for the full 4–12 range, or are there separate age tiers?

## Words we like

explore · discover · investigate · real · actual · scientist · adventure · hands-on · specimen · field · observe · experiment · ocean · habitat · species

## Words we avoid

magical · whimsical · adorable · crafty · simple fun · easy peasy · cute little · unlock · elevate · delve · journey (as metaphor) · revolutionary · game-changing

---

## Hard content rules

- **Never** invent a marine science fact. Flag it for verification instead.
- **Never** invent a testimonial, review, or customer quote.
- **Never** state or imply an educational standard alignment, safety certification, or age rating unless Maya has confirmed it.
- **Never** publish a child's name, photo, age, school, or location.
- **Never** promise a price, discount, ship date, or outcome that Maya has not approved.

---

## Visual direction

**NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT** — Logo files, exact brand colors (hex codes), fonts, photography style, and whether the brand uses illustration, real photography, or both. Until Maya provides these, agents describe visuals in words and never state a specific brand color as fact.
