# Should Sharks Lead? — the recommendation, and the margin tension

**Owner:** Sales Manager · **Prepared:** 2026-09-19
**Question from Maya:** she wants to lead with **Sharks**.
**Metrics involved:** #1 Kits sold · #2 Average revenue per kit · #4 **Gross margin per kit**

---

## The short answer

> ## 🟢 **Yes — lead with Sharks. Sell mixed topics.**
>
> **Sharks opens the conversation. It should not automatically be the whole bulk order.**
>
> Use Sharks as the hook, the sample, the image, the subject line and the first yes. Then structure the bulk order as a **mixed-topic set** — because that is *simultaneously* the better margin and the better product for an institutional buyer.
>
> **And before committing to that permanently: measure the Sharks cost.** The tension everyone is worried about may turn out to be small. Thirty minutes of work settles it.

**CONFIRMED (`company/known-facts.md`):** top-selling kits are **Sharks, Sea Turtles and Coral Reefs.**
**CONFIRMED:** the Sharks kit is **shipping today** — it is the only topic in the catalogue confirmed live (`product-catalog.md` §C).

| Reason | Detail |

> **A hook's job is to get the conversation started. Sharks does that better than any other topic, and it is not close.**

## 2. The tension, stated honestly

**CONFIRMED:** the Sharks kit is **the most expensive kit in the range** — 7 flash cards instead of 5, plus a shark tooth necklace.
**CONFIRMED:** the flash card trial price was **$0.71 per card**, which is a **trial price and a ceiling, not a production cost.**

**ESTIMATE — the card premium alone.** *Inputs: 2 extra cards × $0.71 trial price (CONFIRMED as a trial figure) × 2,000 kits (the goal).*

| | |
|---|---|
| Sharks card premium per kit | **$1.42** |
| Across 2,000 all-Sharks kits | **$2,840** |

**And that is only the cards.** Still unmeasured:
- the necklace components (tooth, cord, clasp, presentation) — **never quoted**
- the **hand-assembly time for the necklace** — **never timed**

🔴 **The labour cannot be estimated and this file will not guess at it.** Necklace assembly time has never been measured, so no agent may put a number on it. **What can be said is that at 2,000 kits, even a small per-kit difference compounds into a large number — and that it may be a bigger cost than the materials.** Timing three necklace assemblies settles it.

**Combined with risk R-02** — *the best-selling kit is also the most expensive* — leading with Sharks at volume does push blended margin down. The concern is real.

## 3. 🔴 But the size of the tension is unknown — and it might be small

**This is the part that should stop anyone deciding today.**

**Open question U-28 is still unanswered:** *is the packed "shark tooth necklace" a real fossil tooth, a replica, or the clay one the child makes?*

`product-development/topics/sharks.md` §C-0 records that the Art activity is **make-your-own from air dry clay**, and that a separate finished necklace *also* appears on the pack list — and nobody knows what that item actually is.

| If the necklace is… | The Sharks premium is… | Then… |
|---|---|---|
| **The clay one the child makes** | Clay + string. **Pennies.** | The whole tension nearly disappears. **Lead with Sharks everywhere, including in bulk.** |
| **A replica tooth, bought in bulk** | Possibly under a dollar | Small. Sharks can carry most bulk orders. |
| **A real fossil tooth, plus hand assembly** | Could be several dollars plus real labour | The tension is genuine and the mixed-topic recommendation matters |

> **Nobody knows which of these is true. That is the actual problem here — not Sharks.**
>
> **The brief that commissioned this work described Sharks as "the worst-margin topic in the range at volume." That is a reasonable inference, but it has not been measured, and one of the three possibilities above makes it wrong.** Sales will not treat it as fact.

**The fix costs about thirty minutes:** price the necklace components, and time three necklace assemblies with a stopwatch. That converts this entire page from a judgement call into arithmetic.

## 4. 🟢 Why "mixed topics in bulk" is the right answer even if cost were no object

This is the part that makes the recommendation easy, and it is worth reading carefully:

**A mixed-topic set is genuinely a better product for a bulk buyer.** Not a margin dodge dressed up as a benefit — an actually better offer.

| Buyer | Why mixed beats all-Sharks |
|---|---|
| **Camp** | A summer has multiple sessions. Sharks week, turtle week, reef week. Repeat campers do not want the same box twice. |
| **Homeschool co-op** | A semester is a sequence of subjects, not one subject twelve times |
| **Teacher** | 5 topics × 5 kits = **five stations and a rotation** for a class of 25, out of 25 kits instead of 25 identical ones |
| **Museum / nature center** | Different programmes, different topics — and topic-matching to their own exhibits is the strongest pitch available |
| **A child** | A collection is more compelling than a duplicate. **The 24-kit completion bonus (CONFIRMED) only makes sense across different topics.** |

> **So the recommendation is not "sell mixed instead of Sharks to save money."**
> It is: **"sell mixed because it is the better offer — and it happens to protect margin at the same time."**
>
> That is the strongest kind of recommendation available: the honest pitch and the profitable pitch are the same pitch.

## 5. How to use Sharks specifically

| Use Sharks for | Because |
|---|---|
| **The subject line and the first image** | It is what makes someone open the email |
| **The sample kit** | Cost is irrelevant at a quantity of one, and it is the best kit to be judged on |
| **The first small order** | Let them start with what they want |
| **A single-topic session set** (a camp's shark week) | A legitimate, deliberate all-Sharks order |
| **The Ring 1 hand-delivered demo** | The necklace is the thing that gets picked up out of the box |

| Steer away from all-Sharks for | Because |
|---|---|
| **Bulk orders above ~50 kits** | Where the unmeasured premium starts to compound |
| **Multi-session or multi-month programmes** | Repetition is a worse programme anyway |
| **Any order where margin is already thin** | |

### The sentence that does the work

Nothing here requires refusing an order. It requires one honest sentence in the conversation:

> *"Sharks is the one everyone asks for, and it's the one I'd start you on. For a whole summer though, I'd mix it — sharks, sea turtles, coral reefs. The kids who come back don't get the same box twice, and you can run them as stations."*

**That is a better answer than "all sharks, please." It also happens to cost Maya less. Nobody has to be told about the second part.**

## 6. If Maya wants all-Sharks anyway — the honest position

**This is Maya's call, and there is a real case for it.**

- Sharks is **confirmed to ship.** The other 23 topics are mostly **NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT** in the catalogue — status unknown, supply lists unconfirmed for most.
- **A mixed-topic bulk offer assumes other topics can actually be produced at volume, and that has never been confirmed.** Supply lists exist for Sharks, Sea Turtles and Coral Reefs only.
- Selling one topic well beats selling five topics badly.

> 🔴 **This may be the deciding factor and it is not a pricing question at all.** If only three topics can be produced reliably, then "mixed topics" means **three**, not twenty-four — and the whole recommendation should be read as **"mix Sharks, Sea Turtles and Coral Reefs"**, which is also exactly the three confirmed top sellers.
>
> **Sales recommends that framing until Product confirms otherwise: the bulk offer is a three-topic mix, not a twenty-four-topic one.** It is honest, it is achievable, and it is built entirely from what is already confirmed.
