# The Path to 2,000 Kits — the honest arithmetic

**Owner:** Sales Manager · **Prepared:** 2026-09-19
**Goal:** D-010 — *sell 2,000 kits as quickly as possible, for as much as possible* (CONFIRMED, set by Maya 2026-09-19)
**Metrics:** #1 Kits sold · #2 Average revenue per kit · #3 Kits committed in bulk · #5 Kits packed per week

---

# The headline, before anything else

> ## 2,000 kits is achievable. It is **not** achievable in weeks — and the reason is not sales.
>
> **Three things decide the timeline, and only one of them is a sales problem:**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **1. Buyers' budget cycles** | Camps buy Feb–April for summer. That block — the largest in the plan — lands **April–June 2027** no matter how good the outreach is. |
> | **2. Maya's hands** | Every kit is packed by one person. 🔴 **How long that takes has never been measured.** It could be weeks; it could be most of a year. Nobody can currently say. |
> | **3. Cash** | Materials are bought before most of the revenue arrives |
>
> **The realistic shape: ~29 bulk buyers, roughly 9 months, landing mid-2027.** In the next 90 days, **200–500 kits and a pipeline holding most of the remaining 1,500** is a genuinely strong result.
>
> 🔴 **And the single thing blocking a firmer answer costs 30 minutes: time three kits with a stopwatch.** Until that exists, the delivery promise, the capacity plan, the hiring decision and the date on this goal are all resting on a guess.

> **CONFIRMED: per-kit assembly time has NEVER been measured.**
>

| If one kit takes | A **50-kit** order takes |
|---|---|
| 10 minutes | **~8 hours** — comfortably inside a week |
| 50 minutes | **~42 hours** — **well outside it** |

> ### Why this is the most important unknown in the business
>
> **A five-fold uncertainty on one order becomes a five-fold uncertainty on the goal.** Scaled to 2,000 kits, the honest answer to "how long does the packing take" is *somewhere between a few weeks and most of a year* — and **nobody can currently narrow it.**
>
> That single gap makes it impossible to:
> - quote a delivery date on a 50-kit order **without guessing**
> - say whether the one-week local promise is safe **at the very order size the floor creates**
> - know whether Maya needs packing help, or doesn't
> - put a defensible date on the 2,000-kit goal at all
>
> Risk **R-05** — *fulfilment depends entirely on one person* — is not a background risk any more. **It is the constraint on the company goal, and it is unmeasured.**

**And Maya cannot pack and prospect in the same hours.** §5 shows the outreach the goal requires. The two workloads compete for the same week.

> ## 🔴 Time three kits with a stopwatch.
> **Thirty minutes. It is the cheapest action available and it unblocks the delivery promise, the capacity plan, the timeline, the hiring decision and the Sharks costing simultaneously.** Nothing else in this document is worth doing first.

**ESTIMATE.** *Inputs: ~29 bulk closes (from §4). Close rate 10–20% of qualified conversations (ASSUMPTION A-028). Reply rate 25–40% to warm, local, personal outreach (ASSUMPTION A-039). No company data supports either.*

> ### 🟢 The 50-kit floor makes this survivable — but not free
> At a 12-kit floor this table read **3 to 10 contacts a day.** At 50 it reads **2 to 6.** That is the difference between impossible and merely hard.
>
> ### Where it still breaks
> **Two to six personalised outreach contacts every working day for nine months — while also packing 2,000 kits by hand, running customer service, and fulfilling existing orders.**
>
> **And the packing half of that sentence cannot even be sized, because per-kit build time has never been measured (§2).** If a kit takes 10 minutes this is tight. If it takes 50, it is not possible at all.
>
> **This is the honest finding of the whole document.** It is not a sales problem or a marketing problem. It is a capacity problem, and it is currently unmeasured.

### The three ways out — Maya's choice

| Option | What it means | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Measure first** | **Time three kits before choosing anything below.** The answer may make A unnecessary — or make it urgent. | 30 minutes |
| **A. Get packing help** | Even one part-time person for pack days. Frees Maya to sell — the job only she can do. | Money, and training. SOPs already exist in `operations/sops/`. |

Raise the floor again** | 20 buyers at 100 instead of 29 at 80. **Maya has already used this lever once, from 12 to 50, and it worked.** Pushing to 100 halves the relationships again — but 100 is above most single camps and co-ops, so it narrows the buyer pool to camps, corporates and multi-site operators. | Fewer possible buyers, higher risk per deal |

**Sales recommends 0, then A, with B as the fallback.** The one thing nobody else can do is be Maya in a room with a camp director. **Packing is the part that can be delegated — but nobody can size the packing job until three kits are timed.**

## 6. 🔴 Could 2,000 be done in 90 days? The honest answer is no

**ESTIMATE — what a 90-day 2,000 would require.** *Input: 2,000 kits ÷ 90 days.*

| Requirement | Reality |
|---|---|
| **22 kits packed every single day, for 90 days, with no days off** | **How many hours that is, nobody knows — build time has never been measured (§2).** That alone should stop the 90-day plan. |
| **~40 buyers at 50 kits each, closed inside 90 days** | Camps do not buy in this window. Schools and districts cannot move this fast. |
| **~$10,000 of flash cards plus unpriced activity materials, bought up front** | Before most of the revenue arrives |
| **A pipeline that does not yet exist** | 0 bulk conversations are currently open |

> **2,000 kits in 90 days is not achievable, and no amount of good outreach changes that.** The limits are physical — one pair of hands, unpriced materials, and buyers whose budget cycles are months away.
>
> **What *is* achievable in 90 days: 200–500 kits, and a pipeline containing most of the remaining 1,500.** That is a genuinely strong quarter and it is the right thing to aim at.
