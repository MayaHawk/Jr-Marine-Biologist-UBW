## Who the decision maker actually is

| Camp type | Who says yes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Independent / private day camp | **Owner or camp director** | One person, one decision. Fastest. |
| County or municipal **parks-and-recreation** camp | **Recreation supervisor or program coordinator** | Real budget, but a purchase order process. Slower, larger. |
| YMCA / JCC / Boys & Girls Club type | **Program or youth director**, sometimes a regional office | Can be one site or many. A regional yes is a very large order. |
| Marine / nature / aquarium camp | **Education director** | Most likely to care about the science being right. Best fit for the product. |
| Church, school-run or after-school camp | **Director or activities coordinator** | Small, fast, low budget |

**The person who answers the general inbox is almost never the person who decides.** Outreach should aim at the director by name where that is public, and otherwise ask for a name rather than pitching.

## What they actually care about — in their order, not ours

1. **Does it fill a real gap?** Every camp has rainy days, bus days, the hour before pickup, and the week the water activity gets cancelled. **This is the strongest angle and Maya has lived it.**
2. **Is it low-prep for staff?** Camp counsellors are often teenagers. An activity that needs a science background is useless to them.
3. **Does it cost less than the per-camper budget line?** Camps think in dollars per camper per week, not in kits.
4. **Will it make parents happy?** A take-home the child is proud of is marketing for the camp.
5. **Will it arrive before camp starts?** Late materials are a camp director's recurring nightmare.

**Notice what is not on that list: marine biology.** They care because it solves their problem. The science is why they trust it, not why they buy it.

## Budget cycle — the honest timing problem

**ASSUMPTION A-022:** camp program materials are typically bought **January–April** for a summer season, with planning starting in the autumn. *(General practice in the sector; not verified for any specific camp. Maya has run camps and can correct this immediately — she is the better source than any research.)*

| Month | What is happening in a camp office |
|---|---|
| **Sept–Nov** | Reviewing last season, planning next. **Reachable and receptive. Not buying yet.** |
| **Dec–Feb** | Programme design, staffing, budget setting. **The decision window.** |
| **Mar–Apr** | Purchasing and prep. **The buying window.** |
| **May–Aug** | Running camp. **Unreachable. Do not pitch.** |
| | |

> ### 🔴 The uncomfortable truth
> It is **19 September**. Camps — the single best-fit channel Maya has — will mostly place orders in **February to April 2027**, for delivery before summer.
>
> **That does not mean do nothing now. It means do the right thing now:** autumn is precisely when a camp director will take a call, and a relationship started in October closes in February. A cold email in February competes with every other vendor; a familiar name does not.

**The near-term exceptions worth working now:** winter-break camps · spring-break camps · after-school and enrichment programmes that run through the school year · Thanksgiving and teacher-planning-day camps. These are smaller, but they are **buying in the next 8–12 weeks**, and they are a way to earn a reference before the big season.

## The offer that fits a camp

> ⚠️ **No price below is confirmed. These are shapes, not quotes. Finance must check margin; Maya must approve any number before it is said to anyone.**

| Offer shape | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| **One kit per camper as the take-home** | The camp's parent-facing win. Scales with enrolment, so the order clears 50 easily. |
| **A season topic set** — e.g. 60 Sharks for shark week, 60 Sea Turtles for turtle week | Matches how camps already structure a summer, and each block clears the floor on its own |
| **A rainy-day reserve** — 50 mixed kits held for cancelled water days | Low resistance, often the first yes — **and exactly at the floor** |
| **Mixed-topic rotation** — 3 topics × 25 kits run as stations | Best margin protection (see `sales/sharks-lead-topic-recommendation.md`) and genuinely better programming |

## The real objections, and honest answers

| Objection | Honest answer |
|---|---|
| "We already have an activity supplier." | Fine — this is not a supplier replacement, it is the gap-filler. Ask what they do on a cancelled water day. |
| "Our counsellors can't teach marine biology." | Each kit is self-contained with instructions. **Do not claim it needs zero prep until someone has actually run one with a teenage counsellor** — that is an untested claim. |
| "What's the per-camper cost?" | 🔴 **Stop. No price may be quoted.** Take the question to Maya and Finance. |
| "Are these safe for our youngest campers?" | 🔴 **Answer honestly and narrowly.** The Sharks kit contains a wearable necklace and there is no product liability insurance (risk R-01). **Never claim a safety standard, certification or age rating.** This objection may be the one that blocks the channel — see below. |
| "Can you invoice us / do you take purchase orders?" | **NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT.** Parks departments and larger organisations often pay by purchase order, 30 days after delivery. Whether Maya can accept that has never been decided, and it is a real gate. |
| "Can you come and run a session?" | An opportunity and a trap. It is Maya's time and it is not priced. **Never offered by a script — Maya only.** |
| "Send me a sample." | Yes, and locally she can hand-deliver it. **But a sample is a real cost** (kit + Maya's time) and needs an approved policy and a cap. |

### 🔴 The two blockers that could stop this channel regardless of how good the pitch is

1. **No product liability insurance.** **ASSUMPTION A-021** — organisations that supervise other people's children commonly require a vendor certificate of insurance. A parks department almost certainly will. This is not a risk-register footnote here; it is a **gate on rank #1.**
2. **Payment terms.** If purchase-order billing is not possible, municipal and institutional camps may be unreachable no matter how good the product is.
