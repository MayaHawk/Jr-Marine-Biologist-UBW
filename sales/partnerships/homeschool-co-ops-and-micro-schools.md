## Why this is the channel with the nearest open window

Camps are the best *fit*. **Co-ops are the fastest *close*.** It is 19 September, and this is the one bulk channel whose buying window opens in the next 6–10 weeks rather than next February.

Three reasons it moves fast:

3. **Maya is one of them.** **CONFIRMED:** Maya is a homeschool mom. In this channel that is not a marketing line, it is membership.

## 🟢 The asset that is currently invisible: the STEAM structure

**CONFIRMED (`company/known-facts.md`, `company/product-catalog.md`):** every kit contains **one Science, one Technology, one Engineering, one Art and one Math activity.**

That sentence is worth more in this channel than anywhere else in the business. A homeschool parent planning a semester is looking for something that covers subjects, not something that is fun. **"Five activities" sounds like a craft box. "One Science, one Technology, one Engineering, one Art and one Math activity in every kit" sounds like a curriculum unit** — and it is the language these buyers already search with.

**The product catalog flags this as under-used. It is currently absent from the sales approach entirely. Fixing that costs nothing.**

> ⚠️ **Hard rule.** The STEAM structure may be described **factually** — it is what is in the box. **No claim may be made that the kits align to any educational standard, meet any curriculum requirement, or satisfy any state homeschool reporting requirement.** That is forbidden by CLAUDE.md §5 and the brand voice guide, and it is also the fastest way to lose a homeschool parent's trust permanently.

## Who the decision maker actually is

| Group type | Who says yes | How money moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Homeschool co-op** (parents teaching each other's children) | The **class teacher** for a science block, or the **co-op coordinator** | Usually a **class fee collected from each family** — so the co-op is not spending its own money, it is passing the cost through |
| **Micro-school** (a small, often 5–30 pupil private school) | The **founder or lead teacher** — frequently the same person | School budget, sometimes tuition-funded |
| **Homeschool pod / learning pod** | One organising parent | Parents pay directly |
| **Umbrella / cover school** | Administrator | Occasionally buys for member families |
| **Homeschool convention or curriculum fair** | Vendor coordinator | ⚠️ Costs money to exhibit, and these are mostly **spring** events |

> ### 🟢 The pass-through insight — this is why co-ops buy bigger than they look
> A co-op teacher is usually **not spending her own money.** She picks the materials and each family pays a class fee. That changes the objection completely: her question is not *"can I afford 20 kits"* but *"will 20 families each happily pay for this."*
>
> **Sell her the thing she has to justify to parents, not the discount.** Her risk is looking foolish in front of twenty families, not the money.

> ## 🔴 The 50-kit floor changes this channel more than any other
>
> **CONFIRMED by Maya, 2026-09-19: free local delivery starts at 50 kits.**
>
> **A single co-op science class is usually 15–30 children. Most co-ops cannot reach 50 on their own.**
>
> Two consequences, and both change the approach:
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Write to the co-op ORGANISER, not one class teacher** | The organiser can commit the whole co-op across classes or year groups. A single class teacher cannot reach the floor no matter how enthusiastic she is. |
> | **Lead with pooling, on almost every email** | *"Two co-ops in the same town usually add up to fifty, and it's the same drive for me."* **Without pooling, most of this channel sits below the floor.** |
>
> **And a single class teacher is still valuable — as an introduction.** Point her at the **$45.99 kit** or the **$9.99 Digital Lesson Pack**, and ask who organises the co-op. She is lead generation, not a lost sale.

| Offer shape | Order size | Clears 50? | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Whole-co-op semester block** — one kit per child per month across classes | 50–120 | 🟢 Yes | **The target order.** Matches how a co-op schedules and clears the floor. |
| **Pooled order — two or more co-ops together** | 50+ | 🟢 Yes | 🔴 **How most of this channel qualifies at all** |
| **Group buy for member families** — the co-op collects, one delivery | 30–80 | 🟡 Often | 🟢 **The best-shaped order in the business.** One address, one payment, zero individual postage. |
| **Single-topic class set** — everybody does Sharks together | 15–30 | 🔴 No | Ships, or pools |
