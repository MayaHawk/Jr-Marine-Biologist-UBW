## Who the decision maker actually is

| Setting | Who says yes | Where the money comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Public school classroom** | **The teacher** | Her own classroom budget, a PTA/PTO grant, a small school fund, or — often — **her own pocket** |
| **Grade level or whole school** | Principal, or curriculum / STEM coordinator | School budget |
| **Private or charter school** | **Head of school, or science lead** | School budget. Far fewer approval layers than a public district. 🟢 **The fastest school buyer.** |
| **Marine / environmental magnet or academy programme** | Programme lead | Programme budget. **Best subject fit anywhere in this channel.** |
| **District** | Purchasing, after curriculum approval | District budget, grants |
| **PTA / PTO** | PTA board | Fundraised money. Fast, small, and often looking for something to spend on. |

> 🟢 **The under-used door: the PTA/PTO.** A PTA is a group of parents with money they have already raised and a standing wish to buy something good for the children. It has no procurement process, meets monthly, and decides in one meeting. **For a Ring 1 school, this may be the fastest institutional money available anywhere in the plan.**

---

## What they actually care about

1. **Does it fit what I'm already required to teach?** A teacher's calendar is not hers.
2. **How much prep does it need?** Teachers are the most time-poor buyers in this list.
3. **Is it enough for a whole class, and what do I do with the kid who finishes early?**
4. **Who is paying?** Frequently the hardest question, and frequently the teacher herself.
5. **Will it survive 24 nine-year-olds?**

> ### 🟢 Where the STEAM structure earns its keep
> **CONFIRMED:** every kit contains one Science, one Technology, one Engineering, one Art and one Math activity.
>
> For a teacher, that is not a feature list — it is **five stations for a rotation, already built.** Twenty-four kids, five activities, one period. That framing is worth more to a teacher than any description of the science.
>
> ⛔ **But no standards claim. Ever.** Not "STEM-aligned," not "meets NGSS," not "curriculum-aligned." Describe what is in the box; let the teacher judge the fit. CLAUDE.md §5 and the brand voice guide both forbid it, and a teacher will trust the honesty more than the claim.

## The real objections, and honest answers

| Objection | Honest answer |
|---|---|
| "I don't have a budget." | The most common answer in this channel and usually true. **Do not discount in response.** Redirect to the PTA, a school fund, or ask when budget is set. |
| "Does this meet our standards?" | ⛔ **Never claim alignment.** Honest answer: "I can tell you exactly what's in it and what it teaches — whether it fits your standards is your call." **Teachers respect this. A vendor who claims alignment they cannot document is a familiar and unwelcome type.** |
| "Is there enough for 24 children?" | **CONFIRMED:** each kit is one child's kit. A class of 24 needs 24, or a rotation. **Be precise — over-claiming here produces an angry teacher and a refund.** |
| "How long does an activity take?" | **NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT.** Never guess. A teacher plans in 45-minute blocks and a wrong answer wrecks her lesson. |
| "Can you invoice the school?" | **NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT.** Schools very often pay by purchase order after delivery. Whether that is possible has never been decided. |
| "We need your insurance certificate and W-9." | 🔴 **The blocker.** See below. |
| "Can you come in and teach it?" | Maya's time, unpriced. **Never offered by a script.** |

### 🔴 Two blockers, both real

1. **No product liability insurance** (CONFIRMED, risk R-01). **ASSUMPTION A-021:** schools and districts commonly require a certificate before purchasing. **This is close to certain at district level.**
2. **Purchase-order payment.** If Maya cannot invoice and wait 30 days, a large part of the institutional school market is unreachable. This has never been decided.

| 4. Get one school using it first | **This is the real step** | Months |

> **Step 4 is the whole game.** Districts rarely adopt a product nobody in the district has used. **The fastest route into a district is one happy teacher in one Ring 1 classroom** — which is exactly what rank #4 produces anyway. The district track is therefore not a separate campaign; it is what rank #4 turns into if it works.
