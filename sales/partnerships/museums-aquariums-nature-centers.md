## Why this channel is underrated

**There are two separate budgets inside one building, and they are held by two different people.**

| Door | Who holds it | What they buy | Typical order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Education / programs** | Education director, camp coordinator, outreach manager | Kits for their own camps, school outreach, scout days, members' programmes | **50–150** |
| **Retail / gift shop** | Retail or store manager, buyer | Product to resell on the shelf | **24–144, reordering** |

The education door is the better one: bigger orders, better price, and **the people behind it are the only buyers in this entire plan who will independently judge whether the science is right — and be impressed when it is.**

The retail door is real but it is wholesale, which means roughly half of retail (**ASSUMPTION A-026**) and **must not be opened until COGS is known.** See `sales/partnerships/bulk-channel-assessment.md` §3.

**Sell education first. Let retail follow if the education side likes it.**

## Budget cycle

**ASSUMPTION A-035:** museums, aquariums and nature centers plan programme purchasing **2–4 months ahead of a programme**, and many operate on a fiscal year that resets in **July or October**, meaning budgets are set months earlier. Gift-shop buying is typically **seasonal, planned 3–6 months out.** *(General sector practice. Not verified for any institution. Any one of them will tell Maya their own cycle if asked directly — and asking is a good opening question.)*

| Near-term windows worth working now |
|---|
| **Winter break camps** (Dec–Jan) — buying **October–November** |
| **Spring break camps** (Mar–Apr) — buying **January–February** |
| **Summer camps** (Jun–Aug) — buying **February–April** |
| **School outreach programmes** — rolling through the school year |

**This channel has a nearer window than camps do**, because winter-break programming is bought in the next 6–8 weeks.
