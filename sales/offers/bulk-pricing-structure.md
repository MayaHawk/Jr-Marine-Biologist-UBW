## 2. The principle that protects "for as much as possible"

> ### **A discount must buy something. If the buyer gives nothing back, it is not a discount — it is a donation.**

Every step down the table should be earned by one or more of these four, **because each one genuinely lowers Maya's cost:**

| What the buyer gives | What it saves Maya |
|---|---|
| **1. One delivery or shipping address** | The biggest single saving in the business. 100 kits to one address instead of 100 parcels. |
| **2. Payment in full, up front** | Cash before materials are bought. This business's biggest structural risk is cash out before cash in (`goals-and-metrics.md`). |
| **3. A longer lead time, agreed in advance** | Packing can be scheduled instead of panicked. Directly protects the delivery promise. |
| **4. Topic flexibility** — mixed topics, or Maya's choice | Lets her ship what is cheapest and in stock, and protects margin on Sharks (see §6) |

**A buyer who wants 100 kits, all Sharks, in one week, paid on 30-day terms, shipped to 40 different addresses should not get the 100-kit rate.** That order costs more to serve, not less. Say so, kindly.

### 🔴 Two rules that matter more than the numbers

**Rule 1 — Do not publish anything below the 24-kit tier.**
Everything from 50 upward should be **quote only**, obtained by asking. Three reasons:
- A published deep discount anchors *every* buyer downward and quietly cannibalises the $45.99 retail price.
- A quote forces a conversation — which is where the local delivery advantage and Maya's credentials actually do their work.
- **A price you have published is very hard to raise. A price you have not published costs nothing to change** once real COGS arrives.

**Rule 2 — No tier may go below COGS plus a margin Maya has set.**
Neither number exists yet. **Therefore the 250+ tiers are genuinely unsafe to quote today**, and the 50–249 tiers are educated extensions of an existing curve rather than costed prices. This is the honest status, and it is why §7 exists.

## 4. 🟢 Local delivery — how to price it without giving the saving away

Local delivery removes **$8.85–$12.00 of postage per kit** (stated range; open question U-8 confirms it). That is **more than the entire 24-kit bundle discount ($6.41).** At the 50-kit floor it is **$442.50–$600.00 recovered on a single order.** The question is what to do with that money.

### Three options

| | **Option A — a separate, cheaper local price list** | **Option B — one price list; local delivery is a free upgrade** | **🟢 Option C — one price list, plus a local concession ceiling** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the buyer sees | Lower prices if you're local | Same price, but *"I deliver it myself"* | Same price; Maya can move down a fixed amount for local orders at 50+ |
| Who gets the postage saving | **The buyer, automatically, always** | **Maya, as margin** | **Maya — unless she chooses to trade some of it to win a deal** |
| Risk | Gives away the whole saving to people who never asked for it, and it is almost impossible to take back | Loses a deal that price would have won | — |
| | | | **RECOMMENDED** |

### Why Option C

The postage saving is **the only genuinely free room Maya has.** Publishing it as a lower price spends it on every buyer, including the ones who would have paid full price for the convenience of delivery in a week.

Holding it in reserve means:
- **Margin improves by default** on every local order — the metric the goal explicitly protects.
- **When a buyer pushes on price, Maya has real room to move** that costs her nothing, instead of cutting into product margin she cannot measure.
- **Local delivery still sells itself on speed and certainty**, which for a teacher or camp director is often worth more than money. *"On your desk by the 10th"* beats *"a dollar cheaper."*

> ⚠️ **Finance must set the concession ceiling** — the maximum Maya may come down per kit on a local order. Sales recommends it be **no more than the measured postage saving**, so that a local order never nets less than a shipped one. **The number is Finance's to set and Maya's to approve.**

### What is delivered with which promise

| Ring | Free delivery at | Promise | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ring 1** — Palm Beach · Broward · Miami-Dade | ✅ **50 kits** (CONFIRMED by Maya) | Hand-delivered, **date agreed with the buyer** | `geographic-rings.md` §3 |

> ## 🚫 Do not quote "within a week" on a 50-kit order
> **Per-kit assembly time has never been measured.** Maya's own illustration: a 50-kit order is **~8 hours at 10 minutes a kit and ~42 hours at 50 minutes** — inside the week, or far outside it.
>
> **No agent may state or imply a build time, and this file does not.** Until three kits are timed, local outreach says *"I deliver these myself rather than shipping"* and **agrees a date with the buyer.** The competitive advantage survives completely; the risk of a repeat of the 50-day failure (open question N-1) does not.
