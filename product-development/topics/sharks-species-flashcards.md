## 🔴 SV-13 — One length to check first

**The dwarf lanternshark is one of the smallest sharks in the world — roughly 8 inches (20 cm), not 4–5 feet.**

The card's own description says so: *"One of the smallest sharks in the world. Tiny organs called photophores produce light on its belly."* A figure of 4–5 feet would contradict the card's own text by roughly a factor of six.

**This may be an extraction artifact** — the "4–5 ft" may belong to the leopard shark, which sits beside it and genuinely is 4–5 ft. **But if the card really does say 4–5 feet, it is a printed factual error**, and the Math activity would teach a child to order the world's smallest shark alongside mid-sized species.

> **Maya is the marine scientist. This needs her eyes on the card, not a guess from text.** Logged in `product-development/science-verification-log.md`.

## 🟡 SV-14 — Sawfish are rays, not sharks

The set includes **Giant Sawfish**. Sawfish are **batoids — rays** — not sharks, though both are cartilaginous fish.

Including one in a shark set is a defensible teaching choice, and a genuinely interesting one *("this looks like a shark, but it's actually a ray — here's how scientists tell")*. **But a card labelled simply as a shark would be inaccurate**, and accuracy is this brand's whole position.

**Maya's call** — she may already have a view on this.
