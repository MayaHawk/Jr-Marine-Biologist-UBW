**Rebuilt:** 2026-09-19 by the Product & Curriculum Manager.
**Built from:** `product-development/card-content/sharks-ALL-extract.md` — verbatim card content from Canva `ALL Making Sense of Sharks Kids Marine Biology Course` (`DAHUeEVgafM`, 20pp), extracted 2026-09-19. **That extract is the source of truth for this file.**
**Format:** matches `product-development/supply-lists/marine-mammals-and-navigating.md` — Maya's own standard.
**Supersedes:** the rough transcription of 2026-08-18. Confirmed prices from that sheet are preserved in **Appendix H**, unchanged.

> ### How to read this file
> - **Quantities are per KIT, not per card.** One glue stick, one ruler and one clay block each cover several activities. **The quantity shown is the most any single card needs — not the sum.** (Maya's stated rule.)
> - **Exception, stated because it matters:** that rule holds for items that are *reused* (glue, tape, ruler, clay). Items that are **used up** — googly eyes glued onto two different projects — must be **summed**. Every summed item is marked in the Notes column.
> - 🔴 **Almost no Sharks card states a quantity.** Where a count is not printed on the card, the figure here is an **ASSUMPTION** and is labelled `ASSUMPTION` in the Notes. Nothing was invented silently.
> - **No prices appear in this file.** Costing is Finance's job and needs real quotes.

## C. Household items — the "what you'll need from home" note

**This is the text that should go in the box, and on the product page.** It does not exist yet.

### Tier A (younger)
> **From your kitchen or craft drawer:** scissors · a cup · a clear jar · water · a pinch of table salt
> **And one more thing:** **about 60 feet of clear floor, hallway, driveway or yard** for the measuring activity. *(A 60-foot run is roughly four parked cars end to end — worth planning before you open card 4.)*

### Tier B (10+)
> **From around the house:** scissors · 6 cups · a blindfold (a scarf works) · a timer or phone · a calculator · a hair dryer or a fan · hairspray or artist's fixative — *adult to apply* · **a room or yard with space to move** · **a helper** for the tracking activity

### My judgement on each Tier B item the Chief of Staff asked about

| Item | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Scissors** | **Household.** | Every home has them; they are an obvious cost to avoid; and Tier A card 1 already marks them not-included. Packing scissors for a 10-year-old also adds a sharp-object question we do not need. **Consistency is the point — right now scissors are marked not-included on one card and not on two others.** |
| **Hair dryer or fan** | **Household.** | Electrical, bulky, and nothing like it fits a 7×5×3 box. No realistic alternative. |
| **A room or yard** | **Household — but say it on the product page, not just the card.** | It is not an item, it is a precondition. An apartment family who cannot pace out an array will hit this mid-activity. Same for Tier A's 60-foot run, which is the bigger ask of the two and is **not currently mentioned anywhere.** |
| **A friend** | **Household — and flag it as a design risk, not just a supply.** | This is the only activity in either tier that **cannot be completed by one child alone.** A homeschooled only child, or a child doing the kit on a rainy afternoon, is stuck. Recommend the card offer a solo variant (a parent, a sibling, or a phone playing a sound from a hidden spot). Flag F-11. |
| **Timer** | **Household.** | A phone. Marine Mammals and Navigating both already treat timers as household — keep the range consistent. |
| **Calculator** | **Household — with a note.** | Every phone has one. But I would ask Maya whether card 5 actually *needs* one: if a 10-year-old is modelling a slow population recovery, **doing the multiplication by hand is part of the maths.** If the numbers are chosen well, the calculator is optional and should be written as optional. Flag F-13. |
| **Hairspray / fixative** | 🔴 **Household, adult-applied — and I recommend it never goes in a box.** | Three separate reasons: **(1)** aerosols are restricted in ordinary parcel shipping; **(2)** it is a solvent spray used by a child on a chalk drawing — an inhalation question I am not qualified to close; **(3)** it can leak or discolour everything around it in a small box. **Better still: make it optional.** Chalk and pastel work can be slipped into the kit's cellophane envelope instead of being sprayed — which is also a neater keepsake. **Maya's call. Safety flag S-5.** |
| **Blindfold** | **Household (a scarf or bandana).** | Cheap to pack, but unnecessary, and packing one invites a child to wear it while moving around a yard. Recommend the card specify the blindfolded child stays **seated or standing still** while the helper moves. Safety flag S-6. |
| **6 cups** | **Household.** | Six cups is roughly the entire volume of the box. Not a close call. |

> **Where this note should live:** printed in the box *and* on the product page. A parent discovering mid-activity that they need 60 feet of driveway is a support email waiting to happen — the same finding Maya made on the Marine Mammals sheet. **Flagged to Customer Experience and Sales.**

## D. ⚠️ Flags — every assumption and inconsistency found

| # | Severity | Flag |
|---|---|---|
| **F-1** | 🔴 | **The card count does not add up.** `known-facts.md` CONFIRMS the Sharks kit contains **7 flash cards**. The Canva `ALL` set contains **10 activity cards** — 5 per tier. At 20 pages and 2 sides per card, the set is exactly 10 cards and **nothing else**. So either (a) one tier's 5 cards ship per kit plus 2 extra collectible cards = 7, or (b) the 7 predates the two-tier structure. **This decides the print run, the pack list, and open question U-30.** `ASSUMPTION` A-041: reading (a). |
| **F-2** | 🔴 | **Tier A card 4 needs "flashcards" and it is not clear which.** "Line Up the Sharks" is a measuring activity; the species list on the back of card 5 (Great White, Bull, Megalodon, Hammerhead, Mako, Tiger, Nurse, Thresher, Goblin, Giant Sawfish) suggests the child lines up **species by length**. That requires **species cards carrying a length** — a printed sheet that **was not found in the 20-page set.** Without it the Math activity has no data. |
| **F-3** | 🟡 | **Old quantities of 3 and 6 probably meant "one per age tier."** Maya's 2026-08-18 sheet lists clothespin ×3, magnet ×3, coffee filter ×3, rubber bands ×3, googly eyes ×6 — across a three-tier structure (4–6 / 7–10 / 10+). Read as per-kit those numbers make no sense; read as per-tier they do. **This file assumes per-tier.** `ASSUMPTION` A-042. |
| **F-4** | 🟡 | **Salt is treated differently in Sharks than in the two finished topics.** Marine Mammals and Navigating both list table salt as **household**. The old Sharks sheet buys a jar. One pinch is needed. **Recommend household** — it saves cost, saves weight, and prevents a leak in the box. Same class of inconsistency as the cotton-ball flag on Maya's own sheet. |
| **F-5** | 🔴 | **Scissors are marked "not included" on Tier A card 1 — but not on Tier B cards 1 and 3.** Exactly the inconsistency the card design standard (D-015) was written to end. Three cards, one rule. |
| **F-6** | 🔴 | **Printed sheets that do not exist: graph paper (used twice) and the fin template.** Same failure mode as the three missing Navigating 10+ sheets. **The 10+ tier cannot ship complete without them.** |
| **F-7** | 🟡 | **Protractor: buy or print?** A printed protractor on the fin-template sheet costs almost nothing and packs flat. A real one feels more like equipment. Maya's call — it is a brand-versus-cost trade, not a science one. |

## E. 🔴 My recommendation on the missing Technology card

**The problem, stated plainly.** `company/product-catalog.md` tells buyers that every kit contains **one Science, one Technology, one Engineering, one Art and one Math activity**, and it uses **Sharks as the worked example**, listing *Sharks' Magnetic Sense* as the **Technology** card. The actual Canva cards label that same card **Science** — so Tier A has two Science cards and no Technology, and the claim the catalog makes is **not currently true of the younger Sharks tier.**

That matters commercially. "One of each STEAM letter in every kit" is among the strongest things this business can say to a homeschool co-op, a micro-school or a teacher, and Sharks is the best-selling topic and the likely lead product for bulk. **Fixing a one-word label is a much smaller job than defending a claim that does not hold.**

### Three options

| | Option 1 — **recommended** | Option 2 | Option 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What** | Relabel Tier A card 1 as **Technology** and rewrite its back to carry a tools-and-tracking frame | Replace the Magnetic Sense card with a brand-new "Build a Shark Tag" Technology activity | Leave the cards alone and stop claiming STEAM for Tier A |
| **New supplies** | **None** | Several — new design, new sourcing, new cost | None |
| **Cost** | One card rewritten; optionally one small printed sheet | A new activity from scratch, plus whatever magnet/clothespin stock already exists becomes dead | Nothing to build, but a real selling point is lost |
| **Risk** | The link must be written well or it reads as a label change | Loses the tier's strongest "whoa" | The catalog and site copy would need correcting |

### Why Option 1, and how to make it honest

The honest through-line is not "magnets are technology." It is this, and it is real curriculum:

> **A shark can sense something we cannot — tiny electric fields, through the ampullae of Lorenzini. So scientists build tools that let *us* sense something *we* cannot: tags, receivers and hydrophones that follow a shark across an ocean.**
> **Technology, at this age, is *tools that extend human senses.*** That is a true, teachable, age-appropriate definition — and it is exactly what the 10+ Technology card (Tags and Acoustic Arrays, term: *hydrophone*) already teaches at a higher level.

So the card keeps its materials, keeps *ampullae of Lorenzini* as its vocabulary word, keeps the magnetic fridge shark as the build — and gains a second half in which the finished shark is **tagged with a number and "tracked"** across the fridge (or across a small printed map) over several days, the way a real tagged shark is tracked.

**What that buys:**
- **Zero change to cost, packing or sourcing** — the thing Finance and Operations most need right now.
- **The catalog's existing claim becomes true** rather than needing to be walked back.
- **Vertical coherence:** a 7-year-old and a 12-year-old both learn about shark tagging, each at their own level. That is a genuinely good argument to a co-op buying two age bands.
- **The "whoa" survives.** "Sharks can feel electricity" is the best moment in the tier and Option 2 throws it away.

**Optional add (Maya's call):** a small printed **tracking map** sheet so the fridge shark has somewhere to travel. It strengthens the Technology frame and is one more sheet in a print run that already needs graph paper and a fin template.

**What I am *not* doing:** deciding. This changes a card in a kit that is already selling, which is outside my authority. **Recommendation only — Maya approves.**

## F. 🔴 The 10+ shark tooth — assessment against the seven design standards

**What is actually in the kit:** Tier B card 1 supplies **one shark tooth, fossil *or* replica**, for a form-and-function investigation with a hand lens, a ruler, sandpaper and fabric. **The younger tier gets no tooth at all** — it moulds one from clay. That distinction closes open question **U-28**, and it matters more than anything else on this page.

### Against the seven standards

| # | Standard | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real science** | ✅ **Strong — conditionally.** Tooth shape versus diet, serration, and the link to *dermal denticles* is real, specific, testable science a 10-year-old can do with a hand lens. **Condition:** every factual claim about the tooth — species, age, what the serrations do — must be verified before print. Logged `[VERIFY]` SV-1, SV-2, SV-4, SV-5. |
| 2 | **Hands-on** | ✅ Yes — it is one of the five things the child physically does. |
| 3 | **The "whoa" moment** | ✅ **This is the best "whoa" in the entire range — if it is a genuine fossil.** A child holding a real tooth from a real animal that really lived, millions of years ago, is a moment nothing printed can match. ⚠️ **A replica is not that.** A replica is a good teaching object and a poor keepsake. **And the copy must never blur the two** — calling a resin replica "a real fossil shark tooth" is the kind of claim that ends trust in a brand built on being real. |
| 4 | **Age-appropriate** | ✅ For the 10+ tier as scoped. ⚠️ **The risk is not the 10-year-old — it is the 3-year-old sibling in the same house.** The tooth must stay out of the younger tiers, and the parent note must say what it is. |
| 5 | **Packable** | ✅ Small, flat, robust, well inside a 7×5×3 box. ⚠️ Two practical notes: a natural fossil is a **variable product** — no two are alike, so marketing photographs must show a typical one, not the best one; and a pointed object loose in a mailer can mark the cards. **Recommend it ships in its own sleeve** — which may be what the five cellophane envelopes were always for (F-14). |
| 6 | **On-brand** | ✅ **As on-brand as anything in the business.** A real specimen with a hand lens is unmistakably a marine scientist's kit and could not be mistaken for a craft box. |
| 7 | **Parent-friendly** | ✅ With one addition: the parent needs a plain line saying what the object is, that it is for the 10+ child, and how to handle it. That line does not exist yet. |

**Verdict: the tooth earns its place — and it should stay.** It is the strongest single component in the range. What it needs is not removal; it is **verification, a tier restriction, a handling note and a sleeve.**

### 🔴 Safety — flagged, not resolved

**I am flagging these. I am not closing any of them, and I have no authority to.**

| Flag | Concern |
|---|---|
| **Small part** | A shark tooth is squarely in the size class that gets flagged for young children. The 10+ child is not the concern; a younger sibling is. |
| **Sharp point and serrated edge** | Real teeth can be pointed and serrated. That is exactly *why* it teaches well — and exactly why it needs a handling note. |
| **Fragility** | Fossil material can chip. A chipped fragment is a new sharp edge and a new small part. |
| **Replica composition** | If a replica is used, it is presumably resin or plastic. **Its composition, coating and any colourant are unknown to us.** That is precisely the category that children's-product testing exists to examine. |
| **Sourcing and provenance** | Fossil authenticity, species identification, and whether the material may lawfully be collected, sold and shipped from its source are all unknown. **Not this team's call.** |
| **No supplier certificates** | CONFIRMED: supplies have been ordered from **Temu**. Marketplace sourcing rarely comes with safety documentation or a traceable supplier (risk R-01, open question U-20). |
| **No product liability insurance** | CONFIRMED in `known-facts.md`. Noted here because a supplied specimen is the component most likely to matter if it is ever raised. |

### What I may and may not say — stated explicitly

- ❌ **I make no claim, in either direction, that the shark tooth does or does not meet CPSIA, ASTM F963, CE marking, any "non-toxic" certification, or any age rating.** **Maya has confirmed no testing of any kind.** Absence of testing is not a finding of unsafe; it is an absence of information, and it must be described that way everywhere.

- ✅ **When anyone asks whether this kit is compliant, the answer is: that needs verification with a children's product safety professional. Here is what I have flagged.**
