# SOP — Getting Orders Out The Door

**Owner:** Operations & Fulfillment Manager | **Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-08-18
**Purpose:** a repeatable routine so no order is ever lost, late, or shipped without the customer being told.

> Built for the current reality: **two order sources** — the Jr. Marine Biologist GoDaddy store (individual kits) and the Under Blue Waters membership (monthly subscription). Because orders arrive in two places, one combined list is essential. That list is the order tracker.

---

## The rule that prevents every fulfillment disaster

> **Every order exists in exactly one place — the tracker — the moment it comes in.**
> Not in your inbox. Not in two dashboards. Not in your head.

---

## A. Right now — the catch-up run (one time)

You have orders waiting. Do this once, in this order:

1. **Open both order sources** — the GoDaddy store admin and the Under Blue Waters membership admin.
2. **List every unshipped order** into the tracker: order number, date, name, email, what they bought, and which store it came from.
3. **Sort oldest first.** The oldest customer has been waiting longest and is closest to asking for a refund.
4. **Flag anyone waiting more than [your normal ship window].** Send those people email **#3 (Running Late)** *today*, before you pack anything. A proactive delay email almost never loses a customer. Silence does.
5. **Pack the oldest orders first.**
6. **Print labels in one batch**, not one at a time.
7. **As each label prints, put the tracking number in the tracker immediately** — not later.
8. **Send email #2 (Shipped) to each customer** the same day.
9. **Mark each row Shipped.**

**Do not start new marketing until this queue is clear.** More orders on top of a backlog makes the backlog worse.

---

## B. The ongoing weekly routine

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| **Every day (5 min)** | Check both stores for new orders → add to tracker → reply to any customer email |
| **Pack day — pick ONE fixed day/week** | Pack everything in the queue in one batch |
| **Same pack day** | Print all labels → record all tracking numbers → send all shipped emails |
| **3–4 days after delivery** | Send email #4 (arrival check-in) — this is your retention and review engine |
| **Weekly (10 min)** | Scan for stalled tracking, unanswered emails, and low stock |
| **Monthly** | Count what's left of each supply → reorder before you run out, not after |

**Batching is the whole trick.** Packing 12 kits at once takes far less than half the time of packing 12 kits one at a time.

---

## C. The packing steps

1. Lay out one of every component for the kit
2. Build kits **assembly-line style** — every card set, then every activity, then every certificate. Not one complete kit at a time.
3. Personalize the certificate with the child's name — **check the spelling against the order**
4. Run the quality check below
5. Seal, label, set aside for pickup/dropoff

### Quality check — before every box is sealed
- [ ] Every activity present
- [ ] Card set complete
- [ ] Certificate included, name spelled correctly
- [ ] Nothing that can leak, crush, or poke through
- [ ] Address matches the order

**One wrong box costs a replacement kit, return shipping, and often a subscriber. The 30-second check is the cheapest thing in this business.**

---

## D. When something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| Out of a component | Stop. Do **not** ship an incomplete kit. Email #3 (Running Late) and reorder. |
| Tracking hasn't moved in 5+ days | Open a case with the carrier, then email the customer **before they ask** |
| Wrong item shipped | Email #5. Fix it first, work out how it happened afterwards. |
| Customer is upset | Reply within one day. Acknowledge before explaining. Escalate any refund decision to Maya. |
| Anything not listed here | Stop and ask Maya. Never improvise on a customer's order. |
