## 🔴 R-01 — Children's product safety and liability exposure

**Status:** OPEN · **Likelihood:** unknown · **Impact:** potentially business-ending · **Owner:** Maya

### The three confirmed facts

All confirmed by Maya on 2026-08-18:

1. The **Sharks kit contains a shark tooth necklace** — a wearable item, in kits sold for children from roughly **age 4**.
2. Supplies have been **ordered from Temu**.
3. There is **no product liability insurance**.

Each is ordinary on its own. **Together they form a stack worth one professional conversation.**

### Why each one matters

**The necklace.** A wearable product for young children sits in a stricter safety category than cards and worksheets — cord length, breakaway clasps, small detachable parts, and sharp fossil edges are all recognised considerations for children's products.

**The Temu sourcing.** Children's products sold in the US fall under the Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (CPSIA). Among other things, that regime involves third-party testing and a Children's Product Certificate, and those obligations generally attach to whoever manufactures, imports, or private-labels the product — not only to the original factory. When components are bought through an overseas consumer marketplace, test certificates and supplier traceability are often unavailable.

> **This team cannot tell you whether CPSIA applies to your kits, or what it would require of you.** That is a legal and regulatory question. What the team *can* say is that the question exists and has not been answered.

**No insurance.** Product liability insurance is what stands between an injury claim and personal or business assets. Without it, the exposure is uncovered.

### Why this is likely cheap to fix

**RESEARCHED RANGE, searched 2026-08-18 — not a quote:**

| Coverage | Typical annual cost |
|---|---|
| $1M general liability, educational toy retailer | **~$300–$800/year** |
| Comprehensive toy business coverage | **~$1,200–$4,000/year** |

Typical product liability limits run $1M–$5M per occurrence. Cost depends on sales volume, product type, and distribution — a small-batch educational kit maker sits at the low end, far below a mass-market toy manufacturer.

*Sources: howtostartanllc.com · thecoylegroup.com · shopify.com · castlerockagency.com · insureon.com · products-liability-insurance.com*

> **This is a researched market range, not a quote for your business.** Only a broker can price it. But at roughly the cost of a few subscriptions a month, this is likely one of the cheapest risks you will ever retire.

### Recommended actions — Maya's decision, every one

| # | Action | Effort | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Get product liability quotes** from a broker who covers children's products | 1 call | Say plainly: educational kits, ages 4–12, includes a wearable necklace, components sourced overseas |
| 2 | **Ask that broker what documentation they need.** Their answer tells you what a professional expects. | included | Brokers know this category |
| 3 | **Ask about CPSIA and children's product testing** — a product safety consultant or the CPSC small business ombudsman | 1 call | CPSC runs a free small-business help line |
| 4 | **Decide about the necklace in the interim** — continue, add a warning, add a breakaway clasp, raise the age guidance, or pause it | Maya only | The team has no basis to choose |
| 5 | **Ask suppliers for test certificates** on anything a child handles | ongoing | If a supplier cannot provide one, that is itself information |

### What this team will NOT do
- ❌ Tell you whether you are compliant
- ❌ Give legal, regulatory, or insurance advice
- ❌ Claim any kit meets or fails any standard
- ❌ Contact a broker, insurer, or supplier on your behalf

### The honest framing
Most small kit businesses operate for years without incident. The point is not that something *will* go wrong — it is that if it did, there is currently nothing between the claim and you. **Two phone calls close most of that gap.**

## 🟠 R-04 — Overseas marketplace sourcing carries supply risk beyond safety

**Status:** OPEN · **Owner:** Operations

Ordering components from a consumer marketplace introduces variable lead times, inconsistent stock, no guaranteed reorder of the identical item, and no supplier relationship to escalate to. For a subscription with a fixed monthly ship date, a component that vanishes between orders is a real operational problem.

**Mitigation:** identify the two or three components most critical to each kit and find a named supplier who can commit to reorders.
